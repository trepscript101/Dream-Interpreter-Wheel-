The Benefits of Exercise for Better Dream Recall

By Patrick Paul · October 11, 2026

In this article I am going to look at “The Benefits of Exercise for Better Dream Recall”. In it we are going to examine sub topics such as:

  • What Is a Dream Recall?
  • Can Exercise Help You Remember Your Dreams?
  • How Can Better Sleep Help Dream Recall?
  • Exercise and Dream Journaling
  • When Should You Exercise?
  • Can Exercise Make You Dream More?

Many people are inundated with the inability to recall their nightly journeys. They woke with the feeling that they were somewhere in the dream state, but can't remember the details.

While research hasn't yet proved that exercise directly impacts dream recall, It has found that regular physical activity such as exercises can make a person sleep well, and since dreaming is related with the quality of sleep a person had, better sleep may give you a better chance to remember your nightly journeys.

What Is a Dream Recall?

According to Carl Llewellyn Weschcke dream recall is the ability to recall the symbols and experiences that occur in dreams. Such information has been used for a variety of purposes, including healing, self-empowerment, and psychological insight.

https://llewellyn.com/encyclopedia/term/Dream+Recall

Dreams mostly occur and are vivid at the stage we know as REM sleep, the brain is very active at this stage. ("Sleep Foundation" (https://www.sleepfoundation.org/dreams))

The problem is that checking the phone, reading emails and doing social media after waking up in the morning contributes to why we forget our dreams upon waking up in the morning.

And dreams can disappear from memory very quickly when you don't pay attention to it as soon as you wake up.

When you wake up during or soon after a dream, you may have a better chance of remembering it.

Can Exercise Help You Remember Your Dreams?

Currently there is no evidence that exercise supports dream recall, however it can aid better sleep.

Many studies and research deals with the usefulness of exercise and the benefits of improving and enhancing sleep quality, but not dream recall. This assertion is backed by a 2021 study where 22 trials were carried out under controlled conditions. The study shows that exercise enhances sleep quality in adults. ("PubMed" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34163383/))

Another 2026 study which was published in the ScienceDirect journal looked at 200 trials involving over 23,000 adults. The research asserts the earlier findings that exercise improves people's sleep. ("ScienceDirect" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079226000110))

These findings are necessary as it establishes that having a healthy sleep makes the brain go through its complete sleep stages.

The same way poor sleep habits can affect regular sleep patterns. Just as regular exercise can improve better sleep condition, this may increase the opportunity of having a good dream recall also.

This is one reason exercise may be useful for people who want better dream recall.

However, it should be noted here that having a good sleep does not equate to a better dream recall. There's need for more research to prove this.

Exercise May Help Reduce Stress

Stress can affect your sleep quality. Exercises are part of the coping mechanism to alleviate the effects of stress.

When you have work, money, family, or other problems and worries, you find it difficult to fall asleep as your mind may remain active going round in a loop.

Enter exercise, which can help improve mood and reduce feelings of stress and anxiety. Regular physical activity is also linked with better sleep quality as we have already establish. ("CDC" (https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/benefits/))

Better sleep may then make it easier to maintain a regular sleep pattern.

However, exercise should not be seen as a treatment for nightmares or disturbing dreams. It is better to think of exercise as one way to support healthy sleep and overall health.

Can Walking Help With Dream Recall?

Do you need to follow a strict gym program or engage in a difficult fitness routine to stay active? No.

A simple form of exercise like walking which can help break a sweat can go a long way to controlling your stress.

You can walk around your neighborhood, walk to a nearby shop, take the stairs, or take a regular afternoon walk.

A 2026 study of the relationship between exercise and sleep included walking among the physical activities that improve sleep quality. ("ScienceDirect" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1087079226000110))

Other physical activities recommended which are also useful includes:

  • Running
  • Cycling
  • Swimming
  • Dancing
  • Jogging
  • Yoga
  • Stretching
  • Gymnastics

A 2024 review examined, and found that several forms of exercise, including yoga, and several traditional Chinese exercises, improved sleep quality compared with no exercise. ("PubMed" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38641082/)

There is actually no single exercise that everyone must use. The best choice is an activity that you can do regularly.

Exercise and Dream Journaling

If part of your goal is to recall your dreams, you should have other strategies beyond exercising, which we have agreed improves sleep quality.

Keeping a dream journal can be very helpful.

It is advisable to keep a notebook beside your bed. That way, it is easier to write whatever you remember from the night’s journey before you lose the memory of it. When you wake up, stay still for a few moments and try to remember your dream before checking your phone or getting out of bed.

You do not need to write an elaborate note. A few words such as how you feel, what you see and who you encounter can help.

For example:

"I was walking through a blue corridor. I saw a black cat which frightened me."

A research by psychologist Denholm J. Aspy evaluates the relationship between dream recall and keeping a dream record. His research found that keeping a log of your dreams can improve the recall of those dreams("PubMed" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27023923/))

Exercise improves sleep quality, while a dream journal helps for a better dream recall.

When Should You Exercise?

There is no single agreed time that works for everyone. It depends on individual preference.

Some people may prefer the mornings, while others prefer afternoon or evening exercise.

The important thing is to choose a time that works for you and does not interfere with your other activities such as work and religious duties.

It is good to know the type of exercise that works for you and the best time to do it. This aids you to fall in sync with regular exercise and a healthy sleep routine.

Doing regular exercises in time can help you discover whether exercise and better sleep make a difference to your own dream recall.

Can Exercise Make You Dream More?

As of today there is not enough evidence that exercise makes people dream more.

Therefore, the safest conclusion is that exercise may support better dream recall by improving sleep, but scientists need more research to confirm a direct connection.

This is an important distinction.

Dreaming and remembering dreams are not the same thing, as you may dream multiple times in the course of one night and yet remember nothing when you wake up.

The reason why we want to improve dream recall is not because we want more dreams. But to become better at remembering prior dreams you already have.

However, apart from exercise, putting into practice the following which we have already talked about can improve dream recall.

Getting enough sleep: Give your body enough time to complete its normal sleep cycles. Which are the REM and non REM sleep cycles.

Keeping a dream journal: It has been accepted that writing down your dreams helps with dream recall.

Staying Calm: Upon waking up, give yourself some time, avoid checking your phone immediately as messages and social media will cause you to forget the dream.

Keep a regular sleep schedule: Going to bed and waking at similar times can support healthy sleep.

Sleep Foundation also recommends thinking about dreams immediately after waking and keeping a journal nearby. ("Sleep Foundation" (https://www.sleepfoundation.org/dreams)

Conclusion

Although research has yet to prove that exercise improves dream recall, regular physical activity such exercises may better the quality of sleep a person has. This gives one a better chance to have improved dream recall.

Exercise such as walking, running, cycling, swimming, yoga, and stretching has been linked with better sleep and reduced stress.

Dream recall is the ability to remember experiences and details from our nightly dreams. Dreams can be forgotten and leave the memory seconds after waking up, so it's recommended to stay still for a few moments to think and possibly log the dream down before doing anything else.

There is really no recommended exercise or time of day to do an exercise. Everyone must follow their own routine and do what works for them. The best approach is to choose an activity that can be done regularly without affecting your sleep routine.
