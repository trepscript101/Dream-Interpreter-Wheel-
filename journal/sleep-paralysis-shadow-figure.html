Sleep Paralysis: What Does It Mean and Why Does It Happen?

By Patrick Paul · October 9, 2026

If you have ever woken up from sleep and felt completely immobile, even though you think you could lift your hands or get up from bed, or try to call for help, but you couldn't when you try to do any of these. You probably are experiencing what is known as sleep paralysis.

This article therefore is going to examine this phenomenon.

The first time I had this experience, I was tired and decided to lay down to take a nap. After a short while I decided to end the siesta and do something else around the house, to my chagrin as I tried to get out of bed I couldn't, it was as though a powerful force held me down on the bed. I tried to struggle with this force to get up or even call for help. Panic sets in when I could do none of that. As I prepared for a possible death, I became free and could get out of bed. This experience traumatized me for a day or so.

This experience of sleep paralysis can feel life threatening, this has made many people question if it has a spiritual connotation. However, medical research explains this as a sleep-related experience that occurs when the brain becomes awake while some of the normal muscle paralysis of sleep is still present.

What Is Sleep Paralysis?

According to an article on the Cleveland Clinic website: Sleep paralysis is a temporary inability to move or speak that happens right as you fall asleep or wake up

It is when you can’t move right before falling asleep or waking up. It's a temporary phenomenon.

As you sleep, your brain helps the muscles in your arms and legs relax. With sleep paralysis, you’re stuck between sleep phases. Finding the source of your symptoms and addressing them can help you avoid future episodes.

Sleep paralysis happens when you regain awareness going into or coming out of rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. Your body hasn’t fully switched sleep phases or woken up during sleep paralysis.

When you’re sleeping, your brain sends signals that relax the muscles in your arms and legs. The result — muscle atonia — helps you remain still during REM sleep. As you’re in the REM sleep stage, you’ll likely have dreams. Your brain prevents the muscles in your limbs from moving to protect yourself from acting dreams out and causing injury.

On a norm our bodies go through different stages of sleep. One of these stages is called rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. REM sleep is strongly linked with vivid dreaming. During REM sleep, the brain temporarily reduces activity in most of the muscles in the body. This is called muscle atonia.

Usually, the muscle paralysis ends before we become fully awake. But sometimes the brain wakes up before the body has returned to normal movement. The person can then be conscious and aware of the room but still unable to move.

When sleep paralysis happens when we are falling asleep it is known as hypnagogic sleep paralysis. In the same vein when it occurs just upon waking up it is called hypnopompic sleep paralysis.

Why Does Sleep Paralysis Happen?

Several factors can trigger sleep paralysis, as researchers are not certain as to why they happen. However, poor or irregular sleep may be regarded as one of the causes.

My experience with sleep paralysis could be traced to my inability to have enough sleep. People who go through irregular sleep periods, work night shifts or travel in different time zones stand the risk of having an episode.

Stress is another trigger. When people go through stress, they become anxious which leads to highly disturbing sleep which may induce sleep paralysis.

Also sleep paralysis has been closely linked with narcolepsy, a sleep condition that can cause long hours of sleepiness especially in the day time which could lead to sudden attack. However, having sleep paralysis does not automatically mean that a person has narcolepsy.

Other factors that have been linked with sleep paralysis include insomnia, irregular sleep schedules, sleeping faced up with your back down, and some family history.

What Does Sleep Paralysis Feel Like?

The experience may be different from one person to the other.

The most common experience which is like what I experienced is the inability to move any part of my body or speak while being awake. Some people can open their eyes and see their bedroom but cannot move their body.

Many people gave account of their experiences, and the following were common:

  • Pressure on their chest
  • A feeling that someone is in the room
  • Fear or panic
  • Difficulty speaking
  • A feeling that they cannot breathe normally
  • Strange sounds
  • Seeing a person, shadow, or other figure
  • Feeling as if they are moving, floating, or leaving their body

Now you would agree that these experiences are frightening and can leave the average person traumatized for days.

Why Do People See Shadows or Feel Someone in the Room?

During sleep paralysis episodes, some people reported a nearby presence or the feeling that they are not alone in the room.

As you may witness a dark figure, hear footsteps, voices, or feel a presence beside the bed.

These may be related to what is known as hallucinations. The Cambridge dictionary defines hallucination as a sensory experience of seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, or smelling something that does not actually exist.

Because sleep paralysis can happen while parts of the brain are still producing dream-like experiences. It may seem you are seeing images, hearing and sounds.

This may explain why a person can see their real bedroom while also seeing something that is not actually there.

The sleep paralysis experience has been attributed to supernatural causes such, ghost, witchcraft, voodoo, and all that in many cultures.

However, science does not support those assertions. Instead, researchers study these experiences as part of the connection between dreaming, REM sleep, and waking consciousness.

Is Sleep Paralysis Common?

In 2011, 35 studies involving more than 36,000 people were carried out and it was found that about 7.6% of the general population had experienced episodes of sleep paralysis at least once. The review shows that the rate was higher among students and psychiatric patients.

However a recent research sees higher estimates of people who have had sleep paralysis experience. A 2024 review examined 76 studies from 25 countries involving more than 167,000 participants. It estimated a global prevalence of about 30%, although the researchers noted large differences between the studies.

How Long Does Sleep Paralysis Last?

An episode of sleep paralysis can last from a few seconds to a few minutes. The episode usually ends on its own, or when someone moves or touches you or speaks to you. Making an intense effort to move can also end an episode. (Harvard health publishing)

Also the Sleep Foundation, agrees that episodes can last from a few seconds to about 20 minutes, with an average of around six minutes. Most episodes end on their own.

Is Sleep Paralysis Dangerous?

Sleep paralysis is usually harmless, even though it causes fear in a person who has just come out from a frightening episode.

Many people are afraid to go back to sleep thinking the experience may occur again.

This can lead to less sleep, and poor sleep may increase the chance of another episode.

And when this happens it can cause a repeated loop. On its own sleep paralysis is not dangerous. But it is advisable to see a doctor if the episodes become frequent. A proper diagnosis could unearth conditions such as narcolepsy or other problems.

Can You Prevent Sleep Paralysis?

There is no known way to prevent it, but adopting better sleep habits may help reduce the risk.

The NHS recommends getting about 7 to 9 hours of sleep, beyond that the following are recommended:

  • Keeping a regular bedtime.
  • Avoiding staying awake all night.
  • Give yourself enough time to sleep.
  • Try to reduce stress before bed.
  • Avoid constantly changing your sleep schedule.
  • Pay attention to whether sleeping on your back seems to trigger episodes.

Since stress is a known trigger of sleep paralysis, adopting necessary coping methods or visiting a physician would go a long way in reducing the occurrence.

What Should You Do During Sleep Paralysis?

Nothing!

Try to relax and let the episode run its course and remember not to panic,

Conclusion

I hope from the foregoing, we have seen that sleep paralysis is not a permanent condition to be afraid of. It is a condition in which a person becomes aware while their body is still partly in the state of REM sleep. This can happen either when a person is falling asleep or waking up and may make a person unable to move or speak. The experience is accompanied by chest pressure, fear, strange sounds, hallucinations, or the feeling that someone is nearby. Lack of sleep or irregular sleep routine, stress, insomnia, and narcolepsy may increase the risk. Although frightening, sleep paralysis is usually harmless and lasts only seconds or minutes. Better sleep habits, regular schedules, enough sleep, and stress management may help reduce episodes.
